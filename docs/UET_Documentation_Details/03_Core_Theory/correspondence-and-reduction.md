# Correspondence and Reduction

This note promotes one of the most important scientific habits in the project:

**A new theory should reduce back to older successful theories in the regimes where those
older theories are already known to work.**

## Why this matters

This is not a minor stylistic preference.

It is part of the correspondence principle in theoretical physics.

If a broader framework cannot recover known physics where known physics is already
successful, that broader framework becomes much harder to trust.

## Why it matters specifically for UET

UET is not trying to become valuable by being different for the sake of being different.

Its credibility depends partly on whether it can do both of these:

1. provide new structure for unresolved problems
2. reduce back to Newton, Einstein, or quantum-style behavior in the correct limits

That is why reduction checks belong to the core theory narrative, not just to scattered
analysis notes.

## The intended reduction logic

The legacy records repeatedly frame the theory as a larger shell that should contain older
physics as inner layers or limit cases.

The intended picture is:

- UET as a broader candidate framework
- known theories as regime-specific successful descriptions
- reduction as a consistency test, not as a defeat of novelty

## What a good reduction claim must include

For a reduction claim to be scientifically useful, it should eventually specify:

- the target theory being reduced to
- the limit or regime being taken
- which variables are mapped to which variables
- which assumptions are being switched off or simplified
- what remains exact, approximate, or heuristic

Without those details, "reduces to known physics" remains a conceptual claim rather than an
auditable one.

## Current posture

The project has long emphasized this reduction idea in its philosophy and internal
interpretation.

That is a real strength.

But the current scientific standard should be:

- keep the reduction goal explicit
- keep the wording conservative
- promote exact reduction claims only where the mapping and regime are documented clearly

## Why this note was promoted from legacy

This idea was too important to leave buried in historical commentary because it directly
shapes how readers should interpret UET:

- as a framework trying to contain older physics where valid
- not as a framework trying to discard all prior theory
